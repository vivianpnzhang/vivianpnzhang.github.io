I got my glider licence before my driver's licence, and my private pilot licence a year later. Now that I'm in my first year of business school, I keep noticing the same ideas showing up in both places.

## 1. Always run the checklist

Every flight starts with a checklist, even on the hundredth flight. **Skipping a step because you "know it" is how mistakes happen.** Good businesses work the same way: clear processes free your attention for the things that actually need judgment.

## 2. Plan the flight, then fly the plane

You plan the route, the fuel and the weather before you leave the ground. But once you're in the air, the wind is never exactly what the forecast said.

> A plan is a starting point, not a promise.

The skill is noticing early when things drift, and correcting a little at a time instead of all at once.

## 3. Communicate clearly and briefly

On the radio, every word counts. Say who you are, where you are and what you want. It's the best training I've had for emails, meetings and presentations.
